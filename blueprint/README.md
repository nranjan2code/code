## What it is

Vakyartha (`vak`) is a local-first agent harness for one owner: agents that work on code, documents, research and everyday tasks, reached from a desktop app, the browser, a terminal and chat apps, with every model input and tool result kept in an append-only record. Its thesis is strong safety, transparent operation, extensible integrations and a simple core.

The lower layers (storage, permission, the pure deciders, the document engines) are well shaped and change rarely. Above them, three large crates (`vak-core`, `vak-agent`, `vak-server`) hold the turn and most features, and that is where most of the work and most of the risk sit.

## Findings

### F1. A turn's decisions share mutable state inside two outlier functions

*Structure · Caused defects*

**Evidence.** Clippy's `cognitive_complexity` scores `Agent::run_message_inner` at 194 (rank 1 of 4,348 library functions) and `Core::run_turn_recorded` at 103 (rank 3); the median is 3 and the 99th percentile 24. 149 commits changed the first (6% of all) and 208 the second (9%). Fix commits land in `vak-core/src/lib.rs` at 10.2 and `vak-agent/src/lib.rs` at 9.1 per thousand lines, against a median of 4.5 over 396 files; file size alone does not predict the rate (ρ = -0.14).

**Why it matters.** The loop keeps the turn's decisions in mutable locals that every step and every call result updates. `948ece43d` fixed two bugs of exactly that kind: `receipts.unresolved_error` was cleared by a later success in the same batch even when an earlier call had failed, and `verification_stale` came out stale or not depending on which parallel result returned first. Both were fixed by moving the decision into a pure function over the whole batch, tested on its own.

**Fix.** Do that for the rest: the turn's state as one type, each decision a pure function of it, and the end-of-turn checks (among them goal mode's audit, the managed-work gate and the commitment hold, which judge durable work at different scopes on purpose) as one ordered list with tests of how they combine. Context planning and the model call can stay as they are.

Sources: Complexity table; `git log -L`; commit 948ece43d; `evidence.py` `hotspots`.

### F2. The rule that wrappers forward every Tool declaration is enforced by nothing

*Structure · Caused a defect*

**Evidence.** The trait's width is deliberate: a tool declares what it is (34 methods, 30 with a default) so that nothing decides by a tool's name. AGENTS.md then requires that "a wrapper forwards every declaration", and no test checks it. 2 wrappers forward by hand, and one that forgets compiles cleanly and answers with the default:

- `ScopedWriteTool` (`crates/vak-agent/src/write_lease.rs:142`): does not forward `card_block`, `card_kinds`, `changes_workspace`, `presents_cards`
- `BrokeredTool` (`crates/vak-tools/src/broker.rs:349`): does not forward `card_block`, `card_kinds`, `presents_cards`

**Why it matters.** `fill_defaults` joined the trait on 2026-10-07 (`fb981ed3d`), and both wrappers began forwarding it only on 2026-10-12 (`53dbc16f8`): for five days every brokered tool lost its argument aliases. The gaps listed above answer correctly today only because no wrapped tool overrides those methods.

**Fix.** A test that fails when a wrapper leaves a defaulted method unforwarded, or a delegation macro that forwards them all.

Sources: `crates/vak-tools/src/lib.rs` (`pub trait Tool`); AGENTS.md; `git log -S` per method; `evidence.py` `tool_wrappers`.

### F3. Storage's guarantees are not tested in the shapes that broke them

*Structure · Defects found by review*

**Evidence.** Owning the storage layer is a decision (docs/decisions.md, 2026-10-18), and it is tested for torn writes, crashes, fsync paths and injected faults, with a fuzz corpus. Nothing tests a chain's reader against a simple reference model, and few tests run two writer processes. The two P1 faults of 2026-10-18 needed exactly those shapes: a cached grant that kept authorizing because its revocation sat in an unreadable segment (A01), and a writer paused between the epoch check and the ref update (A02). A review's probes found them, not the suite.

**Why it matters.** These are the guarantees authorization, erasure and sync rest on. A bug here does not show as a failed turn; it shows as access that should have ended, or a write that should have been fenced.

**Fix.** A model-based test of each chain's reader against a reference, and multi-process interleavings of every fenced write path.

Sources: [docs/audits/architecture-and-presentation-review-2026-10-18.md](../docs/audits/architecture-and-presentation-review-2026-10-18.md) (A01, A02); `crates/vak-storage`, `crates/vak-session`.

## What to keep

- **vak-permission**: 1,166 lines, declared reach, universal Allow, an adversarial escape corpus. Small and central.
- **vak-control, vak-judge, vak-lifecycle**: Pure deciders with no vak dependencies. The right shape for policy.
- **vak-context**: 3,276 lines of pure planning and assembly over the ledger.
- **vak-ooxml, vak-pdf**: No vak dependencies, bounded, fuzzable, run only in the worker.
- **The worker boundary**: A versioned protocol with 14 task kinds; parsers and tools never run in the server process.
- **Effects, runs, cursors**: At-most-once outbound actions and unknown outcomes that are never re-sent automatically.
- **The provenance gate**: Private data plus someone else's words means a human approves anything that leaves the machine.
- **The completion judges**: Whether a deliverable was produced, whether it met its requirements, a run's audited goal, a work contract and a commitment are judged separately on purpose, so produced output can never pass for proof.
- **vak-llm capability declarations**: Wire features declared by the adapter and learned from refusal, never inferred from a provider's name.
